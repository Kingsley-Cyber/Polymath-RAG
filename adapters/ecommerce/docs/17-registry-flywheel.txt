Yes. The **registry/CSV layer is supposed to compound**, but not by simply accumulating more rows. Its value should grow because each high-quality reusable primitive increases the number of future reasoning paths Hermes can recognize without rediscovering them from scratch.

The flywheel should become:

```text
RESEARCH RUN
   ↓
discovers real niche distinctions,
frictions, workarounds, query language,
market bridges, product mechanisms,
successful/failed reasoning patterns
   ↓
RegistryCandidates
   ↓
AUTOMATIC MAINTENANCE TRIGGERS
   ↓
dedupe / recurrence / usefulness / evidence review
   ↓
promoted reusable knowledge
   ↓
NEW REGISTRY SNAPSHOT
   ↓
future research starts smarter
   ↓
better discoveries
   ↓
more high-quality candidates
```

The important distinction is:

> **Registry growth should compound reasoning capability, not database size.**

A new row is valuable when it helps many future runs make a better inference, route to a better source, recognize a useful distinction, or avoid a known failure.


Hermes can do this automatically.

# 9. There are several kinds of market gaps

This should be explicit because **“unmet need” is only one type**.

### `UNMET_NEED`

People have a problem and good products barely exist.

Classic:

```text
market
→ problem
→ solution
```

### `UNDERSERVED_NEED`

Products exist, but they're weak.

```text
market
→ problem
→ mediocre solutions
→ better mechanism/product
```

### `SEGMENT_GAP`

The product exists but not for a particular subgroup.

```text
running product exists
+
family runner / high-mileage / run-club / soldier
has different constraints
```

### `POSITIONING_GAP`

Product works but is framed for the wrong meaning/customer.

This becomes especially useful in Product-Anchored Research.

### `CURATION_GAP`

Products exist individually, but nobody has assembled them intelligently for the community.

This is a Niche Loadout specialty.

### `EXPERIENCE_GAP`

Product technically solves the job but is annoying:

```text
poor access
awkward setup
bulk
compatibility problems
maintenance
bad portability
```

### `VALUE_GAP`

Existing products are:

```text
too expensive
too overbuilt
too cheap/poor-quality
wrong bundle
```

### `IDENTITY / CULTURAL GAP`

Functional solution exists but doesn't fit the desired identity/aesthetic/community.

### `CHANNEL_GAP`

The product exists, but discovery/distribution doesn't reach a specific audience effectively.

### `TECHNOLOGY GAP`

New technology makes an old problem newly solvable.

This connects directly to AI/new-tech paradigms.

# 11. `TREND_LED`

Different from a structural technology shift.

```text

       ↓
```

> Don't sell the trend itself; infer the **behavioral consequences** of the trend.

That's much more Trail-Signal-like.

# 16. Gap Analysis should include **Capture Feasibility**

You raised a very important question:

> Even if demand is being served, how easy is it for us to enter and take a share?

That's not exactly market demand.

Call it:

# `EntrySurface`

or:

# `CaptureFeasibility`

It should analyze:

```text
How fragmented are competitors?

How dominant are incumbent brands?

How differentiated can the product be?

How obvious is the differentiation?

How difficult is switching?

Can people discover it organically?

Does TikTok/content fit the product?

Does it have a visual demonstration?

Can an unknown brand credibly sell it?

Can suppliers create a differentiated version?

Is MOQ practical?

Is margin practical?

How saturated are ads/listings?

Does the niche respond to specialist positioning?

Is the market driven by trust/brand authority?

Are there regulatory/claim barriers?
```

This is exactly how you distinguish:

```text
great demand
but nearly impossible entry
```

from:

```text
moderate demand
but unusually easy niche capture
```

# 23. Fable delta I would add

> **Treat registry growth as an autonomous compounding-learning lifecycle. Every research mode should emit reusable RegistryCandidates whenever it discovers potentially generalizable niche distinctions, activities, frictions, workarounds, physical jobs, query/source patterns, whitespace structures, demand-rerouting structures, reasoning motifs, negative motifs, market bridges or product territories. Ordinary users should never need to request maintenance. Add deterministic maintenance triggers based on cross-run recurrence, unresolved coverage, repeated successful/failed reasoning patterns, query/source yield, registry-support gaps and drift. Trigger Registry Maintenance automatically when thresholds are reached, while preserving immutable per-run RegistrySnapshots and preventing active-registry mutation during research.**
>
> **Add shared `DEMAND_GAP_ANALYSIS` as an integrated analytical operator, not another user research mode. Compare current human/community demand signals against existing market/product/positioning/supply reality and classify gaps such as UNMET_NEED, UNDERSERVED_NEED, SEGMENT_GAP, POSITIONING_GAP, CURATION_GAP, EXPERIENCE_GAP, VALUE_GAP, IDENTITY_GAP, CHANNEL_GAP and TECHNOLOGY_GAP.**
>
> **Add `OpportunityGenesis` so hypotheses are not forced through only `market → problem → solution`. Support at minimum PROBLEM_LED, SHIFT_LED, TREND_LED, DEMAND_REROUTE, CAPABILITY_LED, WORKAROUND_LED and COMMUNITY_LED genesis. Genesis controls research emphasis but grants no evidence authority. Trend/shift hypotheses must still establish downstream behavioral consequences rather than treating popularity itself as a product opportunity.**
>
> **Add `DemandReroute` as a first-class hypothesis for opportunities where underlying demand already exists but can be captured through a new segment, identity, channel, occasion, format, aesthetic, value proposition, bundle or experience.**
>
> **Add evidence-backed `CaptureFeasibility` after gap identification to measure how commercially penetrable an apparently attractive market is. Keep dimensions decomposed—competitor fragmentation, incumbent lock-in, differentiation clarity, switching friction, niche specificity, organic/content fit, visual demonstrability, supplier flexibility, margin room, trust barrier and regulatory burden—and return categorical/auditable routing judgments rather than fabricated market-share probabilities.**
>
> **Feed these structures into Market Discovery frontier ranking, Product-Anchored market bridges, Niche Loadout collection analysis and Opportunity Research qualification/reporting.**

I think this addresses the thing you were sensing: **Trail-Signal should not just search for unsolved problems. It should search for exploitable mismatches between human demand, cultural/technological shifts, communities, existing solutions, positioning, supply and accessibility of market entry.**

And over time, the registry should increasingly recognize those mismatch patterns before the model has to rediscover them from zero.
