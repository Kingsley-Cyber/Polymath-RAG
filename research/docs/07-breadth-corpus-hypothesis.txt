Yes. On a second pass, I think the architecture is safe enough to avoid bad evidence, but there is still one optimization missing if the objective is specifically:

Use a broad second-brain corpus to maximize the probability of generating unusually strong physical-product hypotheses—not merely valid hypotheses.

I would not add another database, graph, or major service. I would strengthen the corpus → hypothesis section of the existing Control Graph.

Do not retrieve corpus knowledge and immediately ask θ for a product hypothesis. What I would add to Fable's implementation plan

I would add only these five things to maximize lead generation without introducing another architecture:

OpportunityPrimitive extraction between Polymath retrieval and bridge generation.
Structural second-pass retrieval using shared predicates, friction families and physical jobs to deliberately retrieve cross-domain analogues.
Diverse hypothesis portfolios instead of immediately committing to one bridge/product mechanism.
Pre-research hypothesis triage based on informational value, distinct from final lead scoring.
Maintenance candidates for successful and failed reasoning motifs, not merely new niches/activities.

Everything else you've built—evidence authority, role-aware satisfaction, physical Product Intake, open-world mode, SQLite durability, registry compiler, and the maintenance lifecycle—already supports this.

The main second-pass conclusion is therefore:

Don't make Polymath better at directly suggesting products. Make it better at exposing transferable behavioral, physical, causal, and constraint structures that θ can recombine into several competing physical-product hypotheses. Then let current-world evidence decide which one deserves to become a lead.

That is how a messy, universal second brain becomes an asset rather than noise: breadth supplies distant mechanisms and analogies; the graph supplies discipline; the loops supply falsification; the web supplies reality.
