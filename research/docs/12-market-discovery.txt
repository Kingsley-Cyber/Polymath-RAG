Yes. I would make this a **third research mode**, upstream of both `OPPORTUNITY_RESEARCH` and `NICHE_LOADOUT`.

But I would not call its primary purpose `MARKET_ANALYSIS`, because analysis implies you already know what market you are analyzing. What you are describing is more valuable:

> **MARKET_DISCOVERY — discover promising market → niche → sub-niche opportunity surfaces, then decide which ones deserve deep investigation.**

Then market analysis is a major stage/report inside that mode.

The key design change is that I would **not choose corpus-first or external-first globally**.

Use **independent discovery lanes**, then merge them. That avoids the biggest hidden failure mode: whichever source goes first anchors everything that follows.

```text
                     MARKET SEED
                         │
          ┌──────────────┼──────────────┐
          │              │              │
          ▼              ▼              ▼
      FIELD-FIRST    CORPUS-FIRST    SUPPLY-FIRST
          │              │              │
  Trends/current     Polymath       Alibaba/product
  web/comments       + registry     category universe
          │              │              │
          └──────────────┼──────────────┘
                         ▼
                  MARKET CANDIDATES
                         ↓
               NICHE / SUBNICHE MAP
                         ↓
                 WHITESPACE SEARCH
                         ↓
                 PROMOTE BEST AREAS
                    /             \
                   ▼               ▼
           NICHE_LOADOUT    OPPORTUNITY_RESEARCH
```

That is stronger than either:

```text
Google Trends → corpus
```

or:

```text
corpus → Google Trends
```

because each path catches things the others inherently miss.

## The three discovery lanes

### 1. `FIELD_FIRST`

This tells you **what is happening now**.

Start with a broad market:

```text
Running
Pets
Home organization
Outdoor recreation
Beauty
Automotive enthusiasts
Parents
Creators
```

Then inspect current signals:

```text
search-interest movement
related/rising terminology
communities
recent conversations
comments
current products
product reviews
current aesthetics
questions people repeatedly ask
things people recently switched to
```

Outputs:

```text
QUERY_CLUSTER
CURRENT_TOPIC
EMERGING_PRODUCT
COMMUNITY
SUBNICHE_CANDIDATE
TREND_SIGNAL
CURRENT_FRICTION
STYLE_SIGNAL
```

This is where a product like high-split running shorts can emerge even though Polymath knew nothing about it.

---

### 2. `CORPUS_FIRST`

This is where Polymath becomes strangely useful.

Don't ask Polymath:

> “What running products should I sell?”

Ask it for **transferable structures**:

```text
behaviors
constraints
adaptations
physical interactions
recurring human tradeoffs
workarounds
identity mechanisms
social rituals
coordination problems
attention patterns
comfort/performance conflicts
```

Then transfer those into the selected market.

Example:

```text
Corpus concept:
equipment layering under external constraint

Invariant:
visible/required outer system cannot change

Possible running transfer:
required/desired silhouette stays,
hidden functional layer changes
```

Or:

```text
Corpus:
behavior around interruption cost

Invariant:
people dislike repeatedly stopping
a primary activity to access an object

Running:
nutrition / phone / water access

Family hiking:
snacks / clothing / water / child items
```

This is the lane that can generate **blue-ocean hypotheses** rather than merely reporting current trends.

---

### 3. `SUPPLY_FIRST`

Your Alibaba category universe gets a very useful second function here.

But Alibaba categories should remain a **supply ontology**, not the definition of a market.

For example:

```text
Apparel & Accessories
Sports & Entertainment
Luggage, Bags & Cases
Consumer Electronics
Pet Supplies
...
```

can be traversed to ask:

> What physical mechanisms/products exist that might solve jobs in this market but are not currently framed for that market?

That's reverse discovery:

```text
PRODUCT / PRODUCT FAMILY
        ↓
mechanism
        ↓
physical job
        ↓
which markets experience this job?
        ↓
which niche/subniche has the strongest fit?
```

This can create excellent unexpected transfers.

But:

> **Alibaba listing = product/supply existence, never market demand.**

Same constitutional rule.

# Then explicitly search for **mismatch**

+

# Corpus becomes more useful at the market level than I initially gave it credit for

I agree with your intuition here.

At individual product validation, current external evidence often matters more.

But during **market ideation**, a large unrelated second-brain corpus is extremely valuable because you're asking:

# The most overlooked design: **query migration**

One thing I'd explicitly guard against is thinking:

```text
keyword declining
=
market declining
```

Language changes.

People may move from:

```text
jogging shorts
→ running shorts
→ split shorts
```

or a brand/product term may replace a generic term.

So Trends research should reason over:

```text
QUERY CLUSTERS
```

not isolated strings.

The market signal belongs to the semantic concept:

```text
minimal high-mobility running shorts
```

which may have multiple query representations.

That's a major safeguard.

# I would therefore add four concepts

To finish the design:

**`MarketLattice`** — markets/niches/sub-niches/intersections without rigid taxonomy.

**`QueryGraph`** — evolving terminology/current topics with provenance.

**`WhitespaceHypothesis`** — explicit mismatch between community/search/product/supply/corpus signals.

**`SignalDivergence`** — differences between channels become first-class discovery signals instead of noise.

Those four turn this from “Google Trends research” into a genuine **latent market discovery engine**.

And the deeper architecture becomes:

> **Current-world sources tell you what is visible. Polymath tells you what could be invisibly connected. Supply sources tell you what can physically exist. The Market Discovery graph searches the disagreement and empty spaces between them.**

That should sit **before** Niche Loadout and Opportunity Research, producing the markets and sub-niches worth spending the expensive deeper loops on.
