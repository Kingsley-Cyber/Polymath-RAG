Yes. This should be a **separate fourth entry mode**, because its epistemic direction is genuinely different.

I would name it:

# `PRODUCT_ANCHORED_DISCOVERY`

UI name: **Inverted Niche Research**

The four modes then become clean:

```text
MARKET_DISCOVERY
"I want to find interesting markets/niches."
Market → niche → opportunity

NICHE_LOADOUT
"I understand the community; build me the right assortment."
Niche → lived world → jobs → 3–6 products

OPPORTUNITY_RESEARCH
"I have an opportunity/problem hypothesis; is it defensible?"
Opportunity → evidence → mechanism → product

PRODUCT_ANCHORED_DISCOVERY
"I already have this product. Who actually cares, why,
and what market/community should I sell it into?"
Product → meanings/jobs → people → communities → niches → market thesis
```

That last direction is fundamentally different enough that I would **not hide it inside Opportunity Research**.

Your example also appears to be **Wenwan walnuts** rather than “jade walnuts”: collectible Chinese walnuts/play walnuts handled as matched pairs. Current Etsy listings position them around hand rolling/exercise, relaxation/gifting, while specialist material emphasizes collecting, pairing, hand feel and development of patina. Etsy currently has a dedicated Wenwan-walnut market surface as well. ([Etsy][1])

That is actually a very good canary because one physical object can plausibly occupy **multiple meanings**, and the system must discover which meanings correspond to defensible ecommerce markets rather than accepting the seller's description.

## The critical concept: don't reverse from product directly to “persona”

This would be weak:

```text
Wenwan walnuts
→ men 30–55
→ meditation enthusiasts
→ sell ads
```

You want:

```text
PRODUCT
   ↓
WHAT PHYSICALLY IS IT?
   ↓
WHAT CAN BE DONE WITH IT?
   ↓
WHAT DOES IT MEAN IN DIFFERENT CONTEXTS?
   ↓
WHAT HUMAN JOB / RITUAL / DESIRE DOES EACH MEANING SERVE?
   ↓
WHO ACTUALLY EXHIBITS THOSE BEHAVIORS?
   ↓
WHERE DO THOSE PEOPLE CONGREGATE?
   ↓
WHAT DO THEY SAY?
   ↓
WHAT NICHES / SUBNICHES EXIST?
   ↓
WHICH PRODUCT↔MARKET BRIDGES SURVIVE CURRENT EVIDENCE?
```

The missing semantic object is therefore not initially `CustomerPersona`.

It is:

# `ProductMeaning`

One physical product can have several possible market meanings.

Conceptually:

```yaml
product_meaning:
  product_id: p_17

  meaning_type:
    FUNCTIONAL |
    COLLECTOR |
    RITUAL |
    IDENTITY |
    AESTHETIC |
    GIFT |
    PERFORMANCE |
    CONVENIENCE |
    HOBBY

  hypothesized_job:
    ...

  hypothesized_behavior:
    ...

  hypothesized_context:
    ...

  authority:
    HYPOTHESIS

  evidence_refs: []
```

That matters enormously.

The same object might be valuable because somebody **uses** it, **collects** it, **displays** it, **gifts** it, **maintains** it, values its cultural tradition, or participates in a hobby/community surrounding it.

Those are potentially completely different ecommerce markets.

# First rule: quarantine the user's/supplier's story

Suppose the user says:

> “I found these Chinese walnuts. Supplier says they're stress-relief health exercise walnuts.”

Store that as:

```text
USER_PRODUCT_HYPOTHESIS
SELLER_CLAIM
```

Not truth.

Particularly:

```text
health claim
stress claim
medical benefit
authenticity
rarity
origin
traditional significance
```

must be independently established before appearing as market/ad claims.

This mode should have an explicit:

```text
PRODUCT_CLAIM_QUARANTINE
```

stage.

Otherwise reverse research becomes extremely susceptible to supplier marketing copy.

# Why Blind Field must not see θ's hypotheses

Same reason as before.

If θ decides:

```text
Product → meditation
```

and then the research agent searches:

```text
"wenwan walnut meditation"
```

it will probably find evidence that appears to confirm its own framing.

Instead one worker gets only:

```text
product identity
aliases
observable characteristics
```

and asks:

> Where does this object naturally appear online? Who talks about it? What terminology, activities, questions, complaints, rituals and related products appear around it?

No hypothesis dossier.

Then separately:

```text
Polymath
→ theoretical/analogical meanings
```

Only later do they merge.

That will make this mode considerably harder to fool.

# Then do `Use–Meaning Decomposition`

θ asks several different questions instead of one:

```text
What does the product physically enable?

What repeated interaction occurs?

What existing object does it substitute for?

What ritual might surround ownership/use?

What skill or expertise changes how it is evaluated?

Could quality differences matter?

Could collecting behavior exist?

Does customization matter?

Does identity/status matter?

```

This produces **competing ProductMeaning hypotheses**.

Do not make them agree.

# Comments become even more powerful in this mode

In `NICHE_LOADOUT`, comments strengthen:


Here they strengthen the inverse bridge:

```text
product → claimed use/meaning → actual people/community
```

So `COMMENT_STRENGTHENER` should be shared but operate with a different objective.

For Product-Anchored Discovery it asks:

```text
Who says they own/use this?

What do they call it?

Why did they buy it?

What did they expect?

What do experienced users care about?

What do beginners misunderstand?

What qualities determine good vs bad?

What frustrates them?

What do they modify/maintain/store?

What adjacent things do they buy?

Is the product primarily functional,
collectible, aesthetic, identity-driven,
gift-driven, etc.?

Where do communities disagree?
```

That gets you much closer to:

> **Who is the actual customer?**

rather than inventing one.

# You need a reverse-fit routing function

Similar to Frontier Utility, but specific to this mode.

For market candidate (n) given product (p):

[
R(n|p)=
w_mM+
w_jJ+
w_cC+
w_lL+
w_tT+
w_dD+
w_eE
----

w_aA-
w_sS-
w_rR
]

Conceptually:

```text
M = product-mechanism fit
J = job/lived-situation fit
C = community evidence richness
L = observed language/identity coherence
T = currentness/search support
D = meaningful product differentiation
E = ecommerce compatibility

A = unsupported assumption distance
S = saturation/commodity pressure
R = redundancy with already-retained markets
```

Again:

> This does **not** mean probability of success.

It means:

> **How much research budget does this product↔market bridge deserve?**

Use diversity-aware frontier selection so the top four aren't merely four names for the same audience.

# Polymath is absolutely useful here

Probably more useful than in simple product validation.

The user provides an object but often has no idea **what latent jobs/meanings could surround it**.

Polymath can ask:

```text
Where else does this interaction pattern occur?

What psychology exists around tactile objects?

What mechanisms create collecting behavior?

What is known about ritual, mastery,
patina, personalization, identity,
progression, gifting, display,
```

But those are **hypothesis-generation primitives**.

External reality still decides whether they belong to this product's actual market.

So:

```text
Polymath
→ expand possible meaning space

Current community
→ constrain meaning space
```

That's the correct relationship.

# Only two real loops are needed

### Market bridge frontier

```text
candidate markets
→ evaluate
→ retain diverse top candidates
→ one deeper sub-niche expansion if gain remains
→ stop
```

### Evidence correction

```text
market bridge hypothesis
→ highest-VOI evidence gap
→ field research
→ revise bridge
→ stop when decision-stable
```

You don't need another simulation loop unless the system decides one of the promoted markets deserves `NICHE_LOADOUT`.

That's key.

## What I would tell Fable

> **Add `PRODUCT_ANCHORED_DISCOVERY` as a separate user-activated mode for inverted niche research. Its input is a physical ProductSeed plus optional user hypotheses such as intended marketplace, expected use, audience, price or source. Treat all user/supplier positioning as hypotheses or seller claims, never evidence. Resolve the product identity and aliases first, then run isolated blind-field, Polymath/registry, current-commerce and optional TrendSensor lanes before merging.**
>
> **Make `ProductMeaning` and `MarketBridge` the central semantic objects. Decompose one physical object into competing functional, hobby, collector, ritual, identity, aesthetic, gift, performance or convenience meanings; map each through interaction → lived situation/job → behavior → possible MarketScope. Do not jump directly from product to persona.**
>
> **Rank product↔market hypotheses with a deterministic reverse-fit frontier utility and diversity-aware selection. Deepen only the strongest meaningfully different market/niche branches. Then discover relevant communities and apply the shared CommentStrengthener specifically to determine who actually uses/owns/discusses the product, how they describe it, why they value it, beginner/expert differences, rituals, complaints, comparisons, adjacent products and contradictions. Use VOI for short targeted correction rounds.**
>
> **The mode must be allowed to reframe or reject the user's initial market thesis. A valid terminal may conclude that the strongest market meaning differs from the user's description, that several distinct markets exist, or that no defensible ecommerce community bridge was found. Preserve rejected and superseded market hypotheses rather than forcing product-market fit.**
>
> **Output a Product→Market Intelligence report with the top defensible market/niche/sub-niche bridges, community intelligence, observed terminology, product meanings, current search/trend context, competition, saturation, positioning, style, product/ad angles, claim boundaries, adjacent-product opportunities and a recommended next mode. A strong market bridge may spawn `NICHE_LOADOUT`; a specific promising product-market pair may spawn `OPPORTUNITY_RESEARCH`; a broader unexpected market may spawn `MARKET_DISCOVERY`.**

The key law for this mode should be:

> **Do not ask “who can I sell this product to?” Ask “in whose lived world does this object already make sense, what does it mean there, and is that relationship strong enough to build commerce around?”**

That prevents the exact failure most inverted market research produces: inventing a customer to justify an object you already want to sell.
